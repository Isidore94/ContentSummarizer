CORE IDEA  
Jesus didn't claim to be God; this conclusion is supported by mainstream biblical scholarship.

KEY LESSONS  
- Historical consensus: Many biblical scholars agree that Jesus did not historically claim to be God.  
- Paradox of belief: Early Christians believed Jesus was God despite belief that he never claimed it himself.  
- Authority given to disciples: In John 20, Jesus grants his disciples the authority to forgive sins, suggesting his own authority comes from God.  
- Walking on water: Jesus walks on water, but Peter does too when he has faith, indicating divine abilities may not be limited to Jesus.  
- Distinction in translations: Different Bible translations (e.g., NIV vs. ESV) reflect varying interpretations of Jesus' divine claims, illustrating how translation philosophies impact understanding.  
- Jesus’s unique relationship: Jesus describes a relationship with God in which he is in the Father and the Father is in him, highlighting a distinction rather than an identity with God.  
- Shared glory and unity: In John 17, Jesus prays for unity among his disciples and himself, implying shared divinity without claiming to be God uniquely.  
- Mistranslation example: The presence of "only" in some translations (e.g., NIV) alters the interpretation of Jesus's claims, pointing to the need for careful examination of original texts.  
- Complexity of doctrine evolution: The development of early Christian doctrine was a collective effort to interpret Jesus’s teachings and their implications, leading to settled beliefs.

EVIDENCE & NUMBERS  
- Bart Ehrman's book "How Jesus Became God" discusses the notion of Jesus's divinity claims evolving posthumously.  
- Brandt Petray's recent work highlights that most scholars still lean towards the view that Jesus did not claim to be God.  
- John 9:10 shows the authority granted by Jesus to disciples, reflecting a structure of belief that does not solely rest on his identity as God.  
- Analysis of John 12:44 reveals significant differences in translating key text, affecting the interpretation of Jesus's statements about faith in the sender.  
- Greek text analysis indicates a difference between "the God" and "God" in discussions of divinity, influencing theological understandings.
