How to Read More Books (And How Not to)
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pm8oXab3zSk

CORE IDEA  
Reading is a valuable tool for learning, but it requires focused effort rather than fragmented moments. Prioritizing deep comprehension over quantity of books ensures effective learning outcomes.

ARGUMENTS & LESSONS  
- A common tip is to carry a book for spare moments. This is ineffective because narratives require sequential understanding to be absorbed fully. Reading in distractions impedes comprehension.
- It is better to read books fully and with focus, yielding greater understanding even if it means reading less overall.
- Time spent on social media can be repurposed for reading. Just one focused hour of reading a week is more beneficial than scattered moments of reading across a month.
- Read books in a focused manner unless it’s a book you wouldn’t read otherwise. In low-priority books, fragmented reading can be acceptable when compared to not reading it at all.
- Categorize books into three boxes: 1) Books you intend to read; 2) Books of interest that you may read in spare moments; 3) Books you have no interest in.
- Dedicate proper reading time for priority books, ideally one chapter per sitting to facilitate comprehension.
- Audiobooks can be effectively utilized if listened to in distraction-free situations. They are equivalent to physical reading when treated similarly.
- Dip into various books at once to manage different priorities without sacrificing quality reading time for essential texts.
- Avoid maintaining a commitment to unfinished books; allow yourself to move on from books that aren't compelling or relevant anymore.
- When reading for specific knowledge, focus on chapters relevant to your goals rather than entire books. This can enhance understanding from diverse perspectives.
- Reread previously assigned school or university books for deeper understanding, as prior experiences may have skewed your perspective.
- Recognize that reading may not be enjoyable if pursued for knowledge rather than leisure. Approach it as a discipline.
- Read with personal goals in mind; avoid the pressure of reading what others find valuable or classic unless it aligns with your interests and learning objectives.

MENTAL MODELS & DEFINITIONS  
- Strategic Information Consumption: The practice of selectively reading portions of a text based on their relevance to your learning goals.
- Knowledge vs. Quantity: Emphasizes the importance of understanding material deeply over simply reading more books.

EVIDENCE & NUMBERS  
- Quality of reading time trumps quantity; spending an hour reading effectively is vastly superior compared to ten minutes across several days.
- The effectiveness of choosing relevant chapters can boost insight more than reading complete books; the example given compares one chapter from multiple relevant sources to multiple chapters of a single text.

COUNTERPOINTS & LIMITS  
- Assumes individuals have the capacity to reallocate time from social media to reading.
- Assumes that fragmented reading will not suffice for learning outside of lower-priority texts.
- Does not provide evidence supporting why some may find joy in what is considered a chore or burden when reading for knowledge.
